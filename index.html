Draw a hero. Then play as it.

Scribble anything you like — a blob, a stick figure, a rocket-cat — and that exact drawing becomes the character you control in a side-scrolling run. It is free, it needs no account, and nothing you draw ever leaves your device.

Play fullscreen ▶ Tap anywhere in the frame to jump. Works on phones, tablets and desktops.

How it works

There are three screens and the whole thing takes about ten seconds to get into.

  1. Draw. You get a paper canvas, a handful of colours, three brush sizes, undo and clear. Draw whatever you want. There is no wrong answer and no time limit.
  2. Play. The game crops tightly to your drawing, turns it into a sprite, and drops it into a running world. Your hero runs on their own.
  3. Tap to jump. That is the entire control scheme. Collect stars, clear pits and spikes, and bounce on monsters.

The controls, in full

One input, three ways to trigger it: tap anywhere on a touchscreen, click anywhere with a mouse, or press Space or ↑ on a keyboard. There is no run button, no duck, and no left or right — your hero stays put near the left edge while the world scrolls past.

There is no double jump. Your hero only responds when their feet are on the ground, so tapping in mid-air does nothing at all. It is the single most common reason a run ends early.

Your drawing changes the difficulty

This is the part most players never notice. When you finish drawing, the game finds the edges of your doodle and crops to them, then scales the result to a fixed height with its proportions intact.

So the shape of what you drew carries straight into the collision box. A wide, sprawling hero has more area to catch a spike, more overhang to snag a monster, and needs a longer clean landing after a pit. A compact, roughly square doodle is meaningfully easier to keep alive.

If a younger player is getting frustrated, the fix is usually not more practice — it is drawing a smaller, rounder hero. If you want a challenge, draw a dragon with an enormous tail. More on drawing a hero that plays well →

Play it offline, or install it

After the first visit the game stores itself on your device and runs with no internet connection at all. You can also install it as an app: open the fullscreen version and use the Install App button, or on iPhone and iPad use Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen. It then launches full-screen from your home screen with no browser bars and no ads.

For grown-ups

No accounts, no analytics, no in-app purchases, no chat, and no way for anyone to contact a child through the game. Drawings are processed entirely in the browser and are never uploaded — which also means they are not saved, so screenshot the good ones.
