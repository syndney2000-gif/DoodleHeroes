For parents and teachers

Doodle Heroes is a free browser game for children. Your child draws a character, and that exact drawing becomes the thing they control in a simple side-scrolling run. This page is the plain version of what it does and does not do.

About the drawings

This is the question we get asked most, so to be completely explicit: when your child draws a hero, that drawing is handled entirely inside the browser on your own device. It is not uploaded, not transmitted, not stored on a server, and not seen by us or anyone else. There is no gallery, no moderation queue, and no way for one player's drawing to reach another player.

The practical consequence is that drawings are not saved. Closing the tab loses the hero. If your child makes one they love, take a screenshot — that is the only way to keep it.

We think that trade is the right one for a children's game. It also means there is genuinely nothing for us to leak.

About the ads

The game is free, and the pages around it carry advertising from Google AdSense, which is what pays for the domain and hosting. We have tried to handle this carefully:

  • No ads inside the game. The play, drawing and results screens carry no advertising at all, so nothing can be tapped by accident mid-jump. On the home page the game sits in a framed area and ads are placed outside it; the fullscreen version at /play, which is what the installed app launches, has none at all.
  • Non-personalised ads only. The site requests non-personalised advertising, meaning ads are not targeted using a profile of the viewer's browsing behaviour.
  • Tagged as child-directed. The site is marked for child-directed treatment, which restricts the kinds of ads that may be served and disables interest-based targeting.
  • Ads are labelled. Every ad slot is marked "Advertisement" so it is clear what is content and what is not.

Ads still involve a third party — Google — receiving a request from your browser, which necessarily includes things like your IP address and user agent. That is unavoidable for any ad-supported site, and it is set out plainly in our privacy policy. If you would rather your child saw no ads at all, an ad blocker will not break the game, and neither will playing offline in installed mode, where the written pages are not involved.

Playing offline

After the first visit the game stores itself on the device and runs with no internet connection at all — useful on planes, in cars, and in schools with patchy wifi.

You can also install it as an app. On the title screen there is an Install App button; on iPhone or iPad use Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen. It then launches full-screen from the home screen with no browser bars, and no ads, because the installed app is just the game.

What it is actually good for

We would not claim this game teaches anything measurable. But a few things about it are genuinely useful:

  • It rewards making something. The loop is draw, then play what you made. Children who say they are "bad at drawing" tend to relax when the drawing is obviously meant to be silly and immediately becomes useful.
  • It has one button. Very young children and children with motor difficulties can play it without a tutorial. There is nothing to learn beyond "tap".
  • It ends on its own. Runs last a couple of minutes at most. There is no daily streak, no energy meter, no notification, and nothing designed to pull a child back. When they stop, nothing punishes them for stopping.
  • The failure is gentle. Losing produces an encouraging message and an invitation to draw something new, not a score-shaming screen.

In a classroom

It works on any device with a browser and needs no accounts, which makes it unusually easy to deploy on shared or borrowed hardware. A few things teachers have used it for:

  • A five-minute drawing warm-up with a constraint — draw a hero using only circles, or only one colour, then play it.
  • A first lesson in game feel. Children can observe directly that a wide character is harder to keep alive than a compact one, which is a real design principle arrived at by playing rather than being told.
  • Offline activity on a cart of tablets with no reliable network — load it once on the school wifi, then it runs anywhere.

If you plan to use it with a class and want the ad-free experience, install it to the home screen first. The game itself has no ads.

Content and safety

The game contains cartoon hazards — spikes, pits, and small purple monsters your character can bounce on. There is no blood, no realistic violence, no death imagery, and no frightening sound or imagery. Losing costs a heart and produces a burst of coloured confetti.

Because drawings never leave the device, there is no mechanism by which a child could be exposed to another user's content, and no mechanism by which anyone could contact them through the game.
